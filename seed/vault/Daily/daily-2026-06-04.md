Counted honestly this morning. Of roughly seven hundred notes, something like four hundred are dated entries, meeting notes, reading logs and fragments — true on a Tuesday and never meant to outlive it. About a hundred are notes I would defend to someone else.

So the vault is mostly stream, and I have been describing it as a garden. [[The Garden and the Stream]] draws the distinction well: the stream is chronological, contextual, disposable; the garden is topical, revised, and improves with age. Both are legitimate; pretending one is the other is not. I keep going to the vault for a considered position and finding six timestamped fragments circling it.

The mechanism of the mistake is that stream entries are pleasant to write and garden notes are work. An entry is finished when the day ends. A permanent note is finished when it says something, and [[Evergreen Notes]] is explicit that the revision is the whole job.

## The plan

- [ ] Stop treating dailies as inputs to nothing. Each one gets a five-minute pass a week later: what here wants to become a note?
- [ ] One promotion a week. One. Not a backlog project.
- [ ] Mark the boundary explicitly in frontmatter so the two kinds stop pretending to be the same thing
- [x] Accepted that four hundred stream notes are not debt and will never be tended

That last one matters. The temptation is to feel behind and start a great retro-processing effort, which fails by week two. The stream is allowed to be a stream — [[Notebooks Are Not Drafts]] makes this point about writers' journals: the notebook is raw material, and treating it as unfinished work is a category error.

The promotion step is the part I have skipped for two years, and it is precisely the distinction in [[Literature Notes Versus Permanent Notes]] — the intermediate form is supposed to be temporary, and mine has become the whole system.

If this works the payoff is the thing described in [[The Vault as a Thinking Partner]]: something that answers back rather than a search over my own past moods. A stream can only tell me what I thought. And the unit of the garden has to be one idea per note, which is [[Atomic Notes]], and which about sixty of my hundred good notes currently violate.
