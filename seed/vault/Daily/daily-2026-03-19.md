Day thirteen with no feeds. Reporting honestly, because the version of this I would write for an audience is already forming and it is a lie.

The withdrawal is real and it is physical. Reaching for a phone that has nothing on it, roughly forty times a day in week one, still maybe fifteen now. It is not boredom exactly — it is a small unmet expectation of a reward, firing on a schedule, which is exactly what [[The Attention Economy]] describes being engineered, and knowing the mechanism does nothing to weaken it. That is the humbling part. Understanding the trap is not an exit from the trap.

Where the hours went, versus where I predicted:

- [x] Predicted: reading. Actual: some, less than expected, and lower quality than I hoped.
- [x] Actual: considerably more cooking, and much longer walks.
- [x] Actual: an unpleasant amount of just sitting there, which I did not have on the card.
- [ ] Predicted: writing. Almost none. This one bothers me.

The sitting-there is the finding. I had assumed the feeds were displacing productive time and they were mostly displacing empty time, which I had never had to be present for. Being bored is much worse than I remembered, and it appears to be a precondition for a good half of what I want.

[[A Thirty-Day Input Experiment]] is where the protocol lives; seventeen days left and I already want to write the conclusion, which is a sign I should not.

The Stoic framing that actually applies is not the grand one. [[Attention Is a Moral Act]] — what I attend to is what I become, and it is the one input I control — is doing more work here than any argument about willpower. And [[Virtue as a Skill]] is why the withdrawal is not a failure: skill has a practice curve, and week two of anything is supposed to be bad.

The bit I am wary of is the temptation to make it ascetic. [[Voluntary Discomfort]] is a training exercise with a purpose, not a badge, and I can feel myself starting to enjoy the deprivation as an identity, which is precisely the failure mode.

If any of this holds up it will be because it changed a Tuesday, not because it produced an insight — which is the whole of [[Philosophy as a Way of Life]].
