Onboarding call with Keiko Tanaka. We have been stuck on this for a month and it ended in about ten minutes, which is irritating in the best way.

The stuck version was a preference fight. I wanted a guided tour, she wanted no tour, and both positions were defensible, so we kept restating them with better adjectives. What she did instead was refuse to argue and write the problem out as a pattern: *a new user has no material, and the app is only legible once there is material, and these forces are in direct conflict.* Then the resolution is not a matter of taste — you manufacture material as early as possible. The tour was never a design choice, it was a symptom of an unresolved conflict, and once the conflict was named the tour had nothing to do.

That is exactly what [[Pattern Languages]] is for and I had been treating it as an architecture curiosity. Naming the forces converts an argument about preferences into a search for a resolution. Christopher Alexander is doing this on every page of *A Pattern Language* — context, forces, resolution — and it works on interfaces because it was never really about buildings.
