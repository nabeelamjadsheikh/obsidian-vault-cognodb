Nine days in April. Practical notes for next time, written while I still remember the parts that will not be in any guide.

## Getting around

The subway is two lines and will not take you most places you want to go. Buses do, and they are slow and crowded in a way that costs you an hour a day if you rely on them. What actually worked: rent a bicycle for the whole stay. Kyoto is flat, the river paths run north–south through the middle of everything, and it turned a forty-minute bus trip into fifteen minutes.

The IC card works on everything and saves fumbling for change on a bus where you pay at the front.

## Timing

- Fushimi Inari before seven. Not eight. By nine the lower gates are a queue, and the upper mountain loop is nearly empty at any hour because most people turn back at the first viewpoint.
- Arashiyama is the reverse — it is unbearable all day and genuinely lovely after five, when the coaches have gone.
- Most temples close at four thirty or five. Plan the day around that and use the evening for walking and eating, not sightseeing.
- Mid-April is after the cherry crowds and before the heat. Two of nine days rained.

## The two places worth returning to

**The Philosopher's Path at dawn**, walked north to south, ending at Nanzen-ji. Empty, and the aqueduct at the end is the single best thing I saw.

**Nishiki market, late morning, on a weekday.** Not for the tourist stalls at the eastern end but for the last third, where the shops are still selling to people who are cooking dinner.

Things I would skip: the bamboo grove, which is thirty seconds long and permanently full. The castle, which is fine.

One regret: I did not book the two places that needed booking, and both were full for the whole week. Do that from home, a month out.
