Something collided on the walk home and I want to get it down before it flattens out into an obvious thing I always knew.

Two notes I wrote weeks apart and never connected: one about how a backlink is useless unless it carries the sentence around it, and one about Richard Feynman's habit of keeping a dozen unsolved problems in his head so that every new technique gets tested against all of them. Walking past the station these fused. The sentence around the link *is* the standing problem. A note that says why it points somewhere is a note that keeps asking a question, and a note that just points is inert.

I have been circling this since November without a handle for it, and the handle arrived in about four seconds while thinking about neither half. Which is [[Creativity Is Combinatorial]] behaving exactly as advertised — not a new element, just two old ones finally adjacent. And the reason they were finally adjacent is that I had both loaded at once, which is the whole mechanism of [[The Adjacent Possible]]: you cannot reach the combination until both parts are in the room.

The condition that made it possible was having a dozen half-finished things in reach rather than one project in focus. That is [[The Idea Compost Heap]] working — the pile is not disorganisation, it is the substrate. And it only pays out if I keep visiting it, which is [[Curiosity as the Engine]] in the boring, operational sense: curiosity is the thing that makes me reread my own unfinished notes for no reason.

Also worth admitting: this happened on a walk, with no goal, which I had not scheduled and would not have defended. [[Play Is Serious Work]] is the note I most believe and least act on. The productive hours today produced nothing; the unproductive twenty minutes produced the thing.

One caveat before I over-generalise. The collision worked because both halves were already sharply defined, and [[Constraints Breed Creativity]] is the reminder that a vague pile combines into nothing — it is the edges of the parts that let them lock together.
