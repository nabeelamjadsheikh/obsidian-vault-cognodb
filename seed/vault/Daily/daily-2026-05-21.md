Rained all day, so I reread my own book notes instead of any books. I expected to feel guilty about this and instead it was the most useful reading day I have had in months.

The reason is that a book note written eighteen months ago is a document by a stranger with my handwriting. He noticed different things. Reading six of them in a row, the overlaps are what jump out — the same idea arriving in four unrelated books, which is invisible when you read them a year apart and obvious in an afternoon.

- [x] Wrote down three cross-book collisions
- [ ] Turn the best collision into an actual note rather than a marginal comment
- [ ] Do this quarterly; put it in the calendar or it will not happen

The collisions:

**Permission to be bad.** [[Notes on Bird by Bird]] on shitty first drafts and [[Notes on Surely You're Joking, Mr. Feynman!]] on the safe-cracking and the plate-wobbling are the same move — Anne Lamott and Feynman both licence work that does not have to be good yet, and both are describing how anything gets started at all.

**Story as container.** [[Le Guin on What Stories Are For]] against the fiction notes: the idea that a story is a bag you put things in rather than an arrow you fire. This one has been quietly reorganising how I think about essays, which is not where I expected it to land.

**Reading as conversation.** Seneca, in [[Notes on Letters from a Stoic]], keeps saying to digest rather than collect — *we should imitate bees* — which is exactly the argument in [[Notes on How to Take Smart Notes]] with eighteen centuries between them. Ahrens is not saying anything Seneca did not; he is saying it with a filing system.

The uncomfortable finding is about my own notes. The best ones are the ones where I argued with the book. The worst are summaries, and there are more summaries than arguments — [[Notes on Meditations]] is nearly all summary and is the least useful set I own, despite being about the book I have spent the most time with. That is a straightforward indictment of how I read in 2024.
