Bad morning. Slept badly, a message landed before I was ready for it, and I spent an hour composing replies I did not send. By ten I had done nothing and was angry about having done nothing, which is the loop that costs the rest of the day.

What actually broke it was not reasoning. I have read a great deal *about* the dichotomy of control and can explain it competently, and none of that helped. What helped was the crude version, said out loud, twice: the message is not mine, the reply is. Thirty seconds. [[The Dichotomy of Control]] works as a tool and does almost nothing as a theory, which is embarrassing given how much of my time with it has been theoretical.

Marcus Aurelius was doing the same thing in *Meditations* and I have consistently misread it as philosophy for an audience. It is not. It is a man repeating instructions to himself at night because he knows he will need them in the morning, which is precisely the argument in [[Journaling as Practice, Not Record]] — the writing is the exercise, not the record of one.

Two things I want to hold onto. First, that this is what [[Philosophy as a Way of Life]] means concretely — a repertoire you can reach for in a specific bad hour, not a position you hold. Second, and less comfortably, the move I made in the afternoon was closer to [[The View From Above]]: I zoomed out until the thing was small, and it worked, and I am slightly suspicious of it. Diminishing a problem by scale is one gesture away from refusing to care, and I do not yet know how to tell the two apart from the inside.

[[Amor Fati]] is the version I trust least and would most like to be true.
