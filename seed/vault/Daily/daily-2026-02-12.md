Spent four hours restructuring the Meta folder. Renamed things, moved things, drew a folder hierarchy on paper, moved them back. Around eleven I sat down to write the thing I had actually opened the vault to write and had no energy left for it.

So: that was procrastination with excellent posture. It felt like maintenance and it was avoidance, and the tell was that I could not name a single question the restructure would let me answer.

The genuinely annoying part is that I already know this. [[Links Over Folders]] exists precisely because folder work is infinitely available and rarely necessary — a folder tree is one taxonomy chosen once, and the reason I keep re-choosing it is that choosing feels productive. The vault has not been organised by folders for two years. I have been reorganising a thing I do not use.

What would have been the right four hours:

- [x] Nothing, apparently
- [ ] Take three fat notes and split them properly, per [[When to Split a Note]], until each one holds a single idea in the sense [[Atomic Notes]] means it
- [ ] Actually write one [[Maps of Content]] page for the systems cluster, which is the only navigation surface I use daily
- [ ] Leave the folders exactly where they are

The MOC point is the real lesson. An index note I write by hand, with sentences explaining why each entry is there, does the job the folder tree was pretending to do, and does it in a way that carries meaning. Folders cannot say why.

Related and less obvious: I moved several notes because their tags felt wrong, which is [[Tags Versus Links]] all over again. A tag is a weak signal that costs nothing and means little on its own; I keep trying to make tags load-bearing and they will not bear load.

Reread [[Why I Keep a Zettelkasten]] afterwards, half as penance. The argument there is that the value comes from the density of connections, not the tidiness of the container, and I have been optimising the container. Adding a line to [[Vault Conventions]] tomorrow: no structural changes without a named question they answer.
