Spent the evening copying an essay I admire, line by line — not retyping the words but rebuilding the skeleton. Every paragraph replaced by a sentence saying what it does. Where the claim arrives, where the example lands, where he lets you off the hook and where he refuses to.

Two hours, and I learned more than from five readings, which is roughly how many times I have read it.

The finding: he does not state his thesis until sixty percent through. Everything before is a sequence of concrete cases that make the eventual claim feel inevitable rather than argued. I had experienced that essay as "clear", and the clarity is structural, not a prose effect. I have spent a year copying his sentences and the sentences were never where the power was.

- [x] Skeleton of the whole essay, paragraph by paragraph
- [x] Wrote my own draft on an unrelated topic into the same skeleton
- [ ] Do the same with two essays I dislike; find what they have in common
- [ ] Stop rereading things I admire and start dismantling them

This is [[Imitation Is Apprenticeship]] in the only form that works — you copy the structure and you do not copy the voice, because the voice is the part that will look like theft and teach you nothing.

It worked because it closed a feedback loop I usually leave open. I knew my essays were worse than his and could not say where. [[The Taste Gap]] is painful, as advertised, but only useful once localised, and "the argument arrives too early in mine" is localised in a way that "mine is worse" is not.

The other lesson is volume. I have written maybe forty essays and dismantled one. [[Quantity Produces Quality]] is usually read as make more things; tonight suggests it applies to analysis too.

Working inside someone else's skeleton was also weirdly freeing, which is the ordinary result in [[Constraints Breed Creativity]]. And it was fun in a way real writing rarely is, which is [[Play Is Serious Work]] again, and [[Seriousness and Whimsy]] — the most serious study I have done in months looked exactly like messing about with a text.
