Reread the first three chapters of *Thinking in Systems: A Primer* on the train, and something finally landed that has been sitting inert in my notes for two years.

Donella Meadows keeps saying that a stock has memory and a flow does not, and I have written that sentence down approvingly at least three times without it doing any work. Today I looked at my own backlog and saw it: the list is not a to-do list, it is a level. It rose all through December while I was working hard, because I was working hard on things that did not leave the list. [[Stocks and Flows]] is a genuinely different way of seeing that, not a restatement of what I already knew, and the version of it applied to project work is now written up as [[Scope Is a Stock]].

> You'll stop looking for who's to blame; instead you'll start asking, "What's the system?"

The other thing that clicked: I have been treating every loop in my life as if it were reinforcing. Deadlines, habit streaks, the compounding-effort story. But most of the loops I actually live inside are goal-seeking — they pull back to a set point, and my frustration is at the set point, not at the loop. [[Balancing and Reinforcing Loops]] makes the distinction cleanly and [[Feedback Loops]] is the general form.

The bit I want to be careful about: [[Leverage Points]] is the seductive chapter, the one everyone quotes, and I notice I want to jump to it and start hunting for paradigm-level interventions in my own week. That is almost certainly the wrong order. You do not get to intervene in a system you have not yet described.
