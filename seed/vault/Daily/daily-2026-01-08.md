Morning pages ran to nearly two thousand words, which has not happened since the summer. Most of it is unusable and that is not the point — I went in with a vague dissatisfaction about the review queue and came out with an actual argument, which is the thing [[Writing Is Thinking, Not Its Transcript]] keeps insisting on and which I keep needing demonstrated rather than believed.

The odd part came later. The same idea surfaced twice in one day from two directions.

- In the pages, working out why my old notes feel foreign when I return to them: I did not write them for the person who would come back.
- In the review queue an hour later, failing a card I have "known" for months and realising I had only ever recognised it, never produced it.

Those are the same failure. Recognition is not retrieval, and a note I can nod at is not a note I can use. Which is the whole force of [[Retrieval Practice Beats Review]] and the reason [[The Forgetting Curve]] flattens for effortful recall and not for rereading.

- [x] Two hours of pages
- [x] Review queue, 40 cards, 6 failures
- [ ] Rewrite the three worst notes so they say something rather than gesture at something
- [ ] Go back through December's entries with an editor's eye

That last one is the actual work. Reading old entries is not review, it is re-seeing — [[Revision Is Re-Seeing]] is written about drafts but applies exactly here, because the entry I wrote in December was written by someone with different assumptions and I should treat him as a source, not as myself.

Which is also the argument for keeping the journal at all. [[Journaling as Practice, Not Record]] — the entry is an exercise, not an archive. The bits worth keeping should graduate into [[Evergreen Notes]] and the rest should be allowed to be scaffolding. I have been unwilling to let it be scaffolding.
