Tried to explain Bolt sessions to a friend over coffee and found the exact sentence where I go vague. It is: *"and then the session handles the transaction boundary for you."*

Everything before that sentence I can say three ways with examples. Everything after it I can also say. That one clause I can only say in the words I first read it in, which is the reliable signature of something memorised rather than understood. Ten seconds of fumbling in front of another human located a gap four months of reading had not.

This is the entire content of [[The Feynman Technique]], which I have always filed as a study tip rather than a diagnostic. It is a diagnostic. It teaches you nothing; it tells you where to go and learn something.

- [x] Explained it badly, out loud, to a real person
- [x] Wrote the vague sentence down verbatim before I could improve it
- [ ] Read the session lifecycle properly, then re-explain without notes
- [ ] Find the other two sentences like it — I know there are more

What made the gap visible was the listener, not the explanation. Alone I skate over it and never notice, because my internal check accepts a familiar phrase as understanding. That is [[The Illusion of Competence]] doing its thing, and the reason [[Learning by Teaching]] works is simply that another person will not accept a fluent noise as an answer.

Richard Feynman's actual rule was harder than the version that gets quoted. Not "explain it simply" but explain it to someone with no reason to be generous, which is why [[Explain It To a Twelve-Year-Old]] is a real constraint rather than a tone.

Two things to keep in view. Rereading the docs is not practice — practice is producing the explanation, which is the ordinary form of [[Deliberate Practice]]: isolate the failing sub-skill and work on that. And this is the plainest case of [[Transfer Is the Whole Point]] I have had all year: I could pass a quiz on sessions and could not use the knowledge in a café.

I explained it well on the second attempt and immediately want to declare victory, which is what [[Scaffolding and Fading]] warns about — the support was the friend's questions, and I have not yet tried it without them.
