Three months of the practice log. Sessions: 71 of a possible 78, which I am pleased about. Measurable improvement over the last six weeks: essentially none, which I am not.

The plateau is real and it is not a motivation problem. Attendance is fine. The obvious culprit is feedback delay: I practise, I feel roughly how it went, and the only honest signal arrives when I test a week later, by which point I cannot attribute the result to anything I did in a particular session. A control loop with a lag longer than its adjustment interval does not converge, it wanders. [[Feedback Latency Sets the Learning Rate]] has moved from "plausible" to "this is my situation".

What the streak is good for is showing up, and that part worked exactly as [[Notes on Atomic Habits]] says it will — James Clear is right that the system beats the goal, right up to the point where the system is running fine and producing nothing. A habit gets you into the room. It has no opinion about what you do there.

Which is where Anders Ericsson is unforgiving, and correct. Time in the room is not practice. [[The Ten Thousand Hours Myth]] is the popularisation he spent years disowning — hours only count when they are structured, targeted at a specific weakness, and *immediately corrected*. That last condition is the one I quietly dropped, and it is the mechanism, not a refinement. More in [[Notes on Peak]].

The other thing I should stop expecting: comfort. The sessions that felt worst in February were the interleaved ones, and those are the only weeks with real gains. [[Desirable Difficulties]] again, arriving as a bill rather than a theory.

And the plateau itself was fine for the first fortnight and demoralising by the sixth week, which is worth noting because the demoralisation is what actually threatens the streak. [[Motivation Follows Action]] holds — the sessions still generate the willingness to do the next one. The willingness just cannot generate progress by itself.
