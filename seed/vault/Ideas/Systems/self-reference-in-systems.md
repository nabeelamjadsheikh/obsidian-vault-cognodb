Luhmann's social systems are autopoietic: they produce the elements they are made of. A legal system produces legal decisions, and what makes something a legal decision is that the legal system treats it as one. There is no external ground. The system reproduces itself by continuously referring to itself, and its boundary is drawn by that reference rather than by any physical edge.

This sounds like word-play until you notice it predicts things. It explains why a legal system cannot be argued out of its own criteria by economics or morality — those are other systems' codes, and they enter only when translated into legal terms. It explains why organisations respond to external shocks in whatever vocabulary they already had.

The connection to his working method is not decorative. Luhmann's slip box was built on exactly this principle: no external index that determines what a note means, just notes referring to other notes, with meaning constituted by position in the network. He described it as a communication partner, which is a strange thing to say about a filing cabinet unless you believe the reference structure is where the productivity lives.

Formally this is the same shape as [[Strange Loops]] — a level that was supposed to describe the system turns out to be inside it, and Hofstadter's account of selfhood and Luhmann's account of social systems are recognisably the same move made in different disciplines.

Two systems-level consequences. Self-reference is how higher-level order appears without anything being added from outside, which is [[Emergence]] with the recursion made explicit. And every self-referring system contains loops whose input is its own prior output, so the whole apparatus of [[Feedback Loops]] applies to *meaning* and not just to quantities — including the delays, the oscillations, and the amplification.
