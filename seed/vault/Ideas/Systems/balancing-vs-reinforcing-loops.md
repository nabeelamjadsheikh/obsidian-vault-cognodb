There are exactly two elementary loop types, and every complicated dynamic you have ever watched is these two interacting.

**Reinforcing.** The stock's level increases the flow that increases the stock. Compound interest, epidemics, reputations, erosion, the rich getting richer. Reinforcing loops produce exponential behaviour, which humans are famously bad at anticipating, and they are always eventually stopped by something — usually a balancing loop nobody had drawn.

**Balancing.** The stock's level reduces the flow that increases it, or increases the drain. Thermostats, hunger, market entry eroding a margin, a hiring plan that fills up. Balancing loops seek a goal, and the goal is often implicit and unexamined. Half the time the interesting question is not how the loop works but who set the setpoint.

Two working rules I use:

- **Behaviour identifies structure.** Exponential growth means find the reinforcing loop. Oscillation means find the balancing loop and its delay — see [[Delays Cause Oscillation]]. A stubborn plateau despite huge effort means find the balancing loop resisting you.
- **Systems that appear stable are usually two strong opposing loops, not one weak one.** Which is why they seem immovable and then move all at once.

Both are just the elementary case of the structure described in [[Feedback Loops]]. The classic tragedy is a reinforcing loop for each individual with the balancing feedback too weak to reach them, which is [[The Tragedy of the Commons]]. And when a measure gets targeted, the reinforcing loop around gaming it overwhelms the intended balancing one, which is [[Goodhart's Law]] stated structurally.
