There are no boundaries in the world, only boundaries in models, and every one was drawn by someone for a purpose. Meadows is emphatic on this and it is the most portable idea in the discipline.

The boundary decides three things at once:

- **What counts as an externality.** A factory drawing the line at its fence produces profit and no pollution. Extend it to the river and the numbers change sign. Nothing physical changed; the accounting did.
- **What counts as feedback versus noise.** Effects that leave and return look like random shocks when the return path sits outside your diagram. Most "unpredictable environments" are loops with a piece outside the box.
- **Who is responsible.** Which is why boundary fights are political but conducted as though technical.

The honest practice is not to find the true boundary but to state the one you chose and check whether your conclusion survives a wider one. If widening the frame reverses your answer, your answer was about the frame.

Optimisation is defined relative to a boundary, so a system can be optimal inside its box and destructive outside it, which is the failure in [[Optimising the Wrong Thing]]. Where you can intervene is bounded the same way, since a lever outside your frame is invisible — half of what makes the list in [[Leverage Points]] hard to use is that the best places to push are usually outside the box the analyst drew.

Properties are boundary-dependent too. Whether a system looks efficient or fragile depends on whether its shock absorbers are inside your accounting, which is the argument in [[Resilience Versus Efficiency]]. And what the boundary excludes is exactly the unmeasured local adaptation in [[Legibility and Its Costs]] — a schema is a boundary with authority.

Whether a phenomenon is visible at all depends on where you cut, since [[Emergence]] only appears above the boundary that groups the parts. Some cuts work because coupling is uneven, per [[Hierarchy and Near-Decomposability]]: cut along the weak couplings and your model is nearly right; cut across a strong one and it is nonsense.
