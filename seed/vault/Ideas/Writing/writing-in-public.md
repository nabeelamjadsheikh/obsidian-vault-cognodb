Publishing before it is finished trades polish for feedback. For most things I write, that is a good trade, and I resisted it for years on grounds that turned out to be vanity.

The argument for waiting is that unfinished thinking is a misuse of the reader's attention, and if you have an audience of thousands that is a real cost. The argument for not waiting is that you cannot tell which parts of your thinking are unclear from the inside, and no amount of solitary revision fixes that. One person saying "I did not follow the middle section" is worth ten self-review passes.

The mechanism I did not anticipate: publishing changes what you notice while drafting. Knowing a paragraph will be read by someone who can disagree makes you check the claim you would otherwise have waved through. It is a forcing function for honesty, not just for polish.

It also solves a completion problem. Half-finished pieces accumulate indefinitely because nothing forces the last ten percent, and [[Finishing Is a Separate Skill]] that only develops if something external ends the piece. Publishing is that something. It pairs badly with perfectionism and well with [[The Daily Quota]], since a steady rate makes any single piece low-stakes.

The distinction that keeps it from being noise: not everything belongs in public. Raw capture is not a post, which is the whole point of [[Notebooks Are Not Drafts]] — publishing the notebook wastes everyone's time, publishing the third pass does not.

The model that fits best is the slow-growing, publicly-revised page rather than the stream of dated posts, an idea I keep coming back to in [[The Garden and the Stream]].
