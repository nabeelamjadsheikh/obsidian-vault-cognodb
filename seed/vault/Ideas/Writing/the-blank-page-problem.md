I used to believe the blank page meant I had nothing to say. Ten years of evidence says otherwise: I always have something to say, I just do not want to say it badly where I can see it.

The blank page is a refusal, not an absence. Specifically it is a refusal to produce a sentence that is worse than the sentences you admire. That refusal feels like integrity and functions like paralysis.

Three things break it, in roughly this order of reliability:

1. Lowering the bar to the floor, which is the entire argument of [[Shitty First Drafts]]. The first version's only job is to exist.
2. Giving yourself a structure to fill instead of a void to face. [[Outlines Are Scaffolding, Not Blueprints]] is about how much structure to allow — enough to start, not so much that you stop thinking.
3. Making the decision in advance so it is not made at the desk. [[The Daily Quota]] works because it converts a daily act of courage into a daily act of compliance.

There is one more trick that is really a reframe. The opening line feels heavy because [[The First Sentence Is a Contract]] with the reader — so write it last. Start in the middle, where no promises are being made, and let the beginning be a thing you assemble once you know what you have promised.

The page is not blank because you are empty. It is blank because you have not yet agreed to be embarrassing in private.
