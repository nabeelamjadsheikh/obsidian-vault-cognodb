Not all jargon is the same, and collapsing the categories makes for lazy criticism.

**Load-bearing jargon** genuinely compresses. "Idempotent" replaces a sentence, and among people who know it, the replacement is exact. Removing it would make the prose longer and less precise. Fields need this, and demanding its removal is demanding that specialists talk like tourists.

**Decorative jargon** is a bookmark where understanding should be. The writer reaches for the term because it is the shape of the thing they were told, not because they could unpack it. This is what [[Explain It To a Twelve-Year-Old]] detects — try to say it without the word and the vagueness becomes visible immediately.

**Sorting jargon** exists to separate readers into worthy and unworthy. It rarely announces itself. The tell is that removing it would cost no precision at all and only cost the writer some status. Whole subfields run on this, and the participants would sincerely deny it.

The uncomfortable part is that from the outside these are nearly indistinguishable, and from the inside [[The Curse of Knowledge]] guarantees you will misclassify your own. Everyone believes their jargon is load-bearing. The only real test is whether you can produce the unpacked version on demand, and whether you ever do.

My working rule: use the term, then immediately pay for it. One clause of plain-language gloss on first use. It costs eight words and it converts sorting jargon into teaching, because it addresses the actual person on the other side, which is what [[Write For One Reader]] is for and what [[Plain Language Is a Form of Respect]] demands.
