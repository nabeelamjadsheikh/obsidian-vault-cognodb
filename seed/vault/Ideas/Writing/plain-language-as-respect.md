Keiko said something in a design review that reorganised how I think about prose. We were arguing about an empty state, and she said: every ambiguity you leave in gets multiplied by the number of people who read it. You are choosing between spending an hour once and spending thirty seconds a thousand times.

That is the whole ethic. Clarity is a transfer of cost from the reader to the writer. Difficult prose has not eliminated the work of understanding; it has exported it, unpriced, to everyone downstream.

Which reframes the usual defences. "The subject is genuinely complex" is often true and almost never a complete answer, because the question is not whether complexity exists but who is made to carry it. "This is how the field writes" is a description of a norm, not a justification of it — and frequently the norm is doing other work entirely, the sorting function described in [[Jargon as Gatekeeping]].

Practically, plain does not mean short or simple. It means:

- The sentence's grammatical subject is the thing actually doing something.
- Terms are introduced before they are used, not after.
- The reader is never asked to hold three things in mind to parse a fourth.

Two habits get me most of the way there. [[Read It Aloud]], because prose that cannot be spoken is prose that is making the reader work to breathe. And addressing one specific person, as in [[Write For One Reader]], which sets the level of explanation automatically instead of leaving it to a rule you will forget.

Respect is the right word. Obscurity is a way of not caring what happens after you hit publish.
