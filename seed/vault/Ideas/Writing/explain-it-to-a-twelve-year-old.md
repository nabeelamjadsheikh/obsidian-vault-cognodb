The story, possibly apocryphal in its details but true in its spirit: Feynman was asked to prepare a freshman lecture explaining why spin-one-half particles obey Fermi-Dirac statistics. He came back and said he could not do it — he could not reduce it to the freshman level, and that meant we do not really understand it.

That is the whole standard, and it is brutal. Not "explain it simply" as a communication exercise, but "the inability to explain it simply is diagnostic of your own understanding." The vagueness in your explanation is not in the explanation. It is in you.

I use it as a debugging tool rather than a writing style. Draft the explanation for a bright twelve-year-old, and watch for the places where you reach for a term instead of a picture. Each of those is a hole. Half the time the term is load-bearing compression and fine; the other half it is a bookmark where understanding should be, which is exactly the distinction drawn in [[Jargon as Gatekeeping]].

The reason this is hard is [[The Curse of Knowledge]]: you cannot detect the missing steps by inspection, but you can detect them by trying to say the thing without the vocabulary that hides them.

One caveat I have grown into. Simplifying is not the same as dumbing down, and it costs the writer real time to do it well. Spending that time so the reader does not have to is the whole ethic of [[Plain Language Is a Form of Respect]].
