The solution shows up in the shower, on the walk, in the ninety seconds before sleep. Never at the desk. This is so common that people treat it as a joke about plumbing, and it is actually a fact about attention.

Focused attention travels along established paths. That is what makes it useful — it is how you execute — and it is also why it cannot reframe a problem. Being stuck almost always means the framing forbids the answer, and more focus keeps you inside the framing. The shower works because it is one of the few remaining places where you cannot steer, cannot check anything, and have nothing to do. Oakley's version of the mechanism is in the alternation described in [[Diffuse and Focused Modes]].

Two conditions, and both are required:

**You must load the problem first.** Incubation works on what you recently worked hard on. Idle time with nothing loaded is just idle time. The romance about walks omits that the walk only pays after the struggle.

**You must actually stop steering.** A podcast in the shower is not incubation. Neither is switching to another demanding task. This is why boredom has become scarce and worth defending — the argument in [[Boredom Is a Signal]] is that the empty state is doing work, not wasting time.

Which reframes what a creative block usually is. If the solution arrives when you stop pushing, then pushing harder against a block is precisely wrong, and the block itself is rarely emptiness anyway — see [[Creative Block Is Usually Fear]].

The practical setup is to have several problems loaded at once and something to catch the output, since the return is unscheduled and lasts about forty seconds. That is one of the reasons I keep [[The Idea Compost Heap]] where I can reach it wet-handed.
