A block feels like emptiness. Every time I have examined one honestly, it has been full — full of work I was unwilling to do badly.

Lamott's whole method is built on this diagnosis. Her radio station KFKD, playing self-congratulation in one ear and contempt in the other, is not a metaphor about creativity; it is a description of the anxiety that stops the hand. Her prescriptions are all anxiety management rather than technique: the one-inch picture frame, the explicit permission to write appallingly.

The tells, which I now recognise:

- Blocked on the piece, fluent in an email about the piece. There is no capacity problem. There is an exposure problem.
- The block is worst on the work that matters most, which is exactly backwards if it were about ability and exactly right if it were about stakes.
- Research feels urgent. Research is the most respectable form of not writing available.

Two things reliably dissolve it. Lowering the standard for the current attempt until it is unmistakably not a verdict — the volume argument in [[Quantity Produces Quality]] is the same medicine, since the fortieth attempt this year cannot carry the weight the first one did. And genuinely stopping, which is not the same as forcing: if the answer arrives when you leave, as in [[Incubation and the Shower Effect]], then grinding against a block is the one guaranteed way to prevent the thing you want.

The underlying condition is that your taste is better than your hands and you are working in full view of your own judgment. That is permanent, it is the subject of [[The Taste Gap]], and treating it as an emergency rather than as the ordinary weather of the work is what turns a bad afternoon into a blocked year.
