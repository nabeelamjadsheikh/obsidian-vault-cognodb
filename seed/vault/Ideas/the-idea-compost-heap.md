Half-formed ideas are not failures in need of resolution. They are material that needs to rot for a season, near other material, before anything grows out of them.

The heap in my vault is a real thing: fragments, unresolved observations, an argument I cannot finish, three quotations that seem related for reasons I cannot state, a question I asked in February and did not answer. Nothing in it is presentable. Its only rule is that everything lives together rather than in tidy separate folders, because the whole value depends on adjacency.

Why it works, in two parts.

**Time.** A fragment written in March is unreadable to me in April and suddenly obvious in September, because I have acquired the missing piece in between. Nothing about the fragment changed; my position changed, which is the mechanism in [[The Adjacent Possible]]. Resolving everything immediately destroys this by forcing a conclusion at the moment of least information.

**Proximity.** Ideas that never meet never combine, and the combination rate is the thing that actually determines output, per [[Creativity Is Combinatorial]]. A well-organised archive with each thought in its correct folder is optimised against exactly this.

The heap is also where incubation returns its results. The forty seconds after a solution appears in the shower need somewhere with zero friction to receive it, which is the practical requirement behind [[Incubation and the Shower Effect]].

Two disciplines keep it from becoming landfill. Things go in against a live question, per [[Collecting With Intent]], and I turn the heap over every few weeks — reading, not sorting. Half of what I find is dead, which is fine. Compost is mostly the stuff that did not become a plant.

The other thing it does is remove the pressure to be right at the moment of writing, which is where a surprising amount of blocked work actually comes from — see [[Creative Block Is Usually Fear]].
