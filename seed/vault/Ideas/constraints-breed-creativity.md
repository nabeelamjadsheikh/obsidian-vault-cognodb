Tell someone to write anything and they freeze. Tell them to write six words about a shoe and they produce something. The constraint did not limit the space of good outcomes; it made the space navigable.

The reason is that an unbounded problem has no gradient. Every direction is equally permitted, so nothing suggests the next move, and effort has nothing to climb. A constraint creates slope. It converts an open field into a maze, and mazes can be solved.

The second effect is more interesting than the first. Constraints force you off the obvious path, and the obvious path is where everyone's first three ideas live. If you cannot use the word, or the colour, or the extra week, you must reach for something further from the centre of your habits — which is a mechanical way of increasing the reach of the recombination described in [[Creativity Is Combinatorial]]. The constraint pushes the search into regions you would never have visited voluntarily.

Not every constraint helps. The useful ones bind the **form** and leave the content free: fixed length, fixed materials, fixed time, a rule about what is forbidden. The destructive ones bind the outcome — do anything you like as long as the result is this — which removes the search entirely.

Two related notes. Constraints are also what makes the whole thing safe to attempt, because a bounded exercise is obviously a game and games license failure, which is the argument in [[Play Is Serious Work]]. And a self-imposed constraint is the most reliable way I know to reach the parts of the space adjacent to where you already are, per [[The Adjacent Possible]].

The last argument is temperamental. Arbitrary rules are funny, and taking a silly constraint seriously is exactly the posture described in [[Seriousness and Whimsy]] — solemn people cannot use this technique at all.
