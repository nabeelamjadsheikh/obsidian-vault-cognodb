The single best predictor of how fast someone gets good at something is not effort, talent, or hours. It is how long they wait to find out whether what they did worked.

Compare guitar and management. A wrong note is audible in fifty milliseconds; you correct within the bar and get thousands of corrections an hour. A bad hiring decision reveals itself in nine months, confounded by twenty other variables, and by then you have made forty more decisions on the same faulty model. People do not become slowly better managers because they are lazier than guitarists. The signal is late and noisy.

Two things follow.

**Slow feedback breeds superstition.** When the outcome arrives long after the action, you cannot attribute it correctly, so you attribute it to whatever is salient. Every domain with a nine-month loop has an elaborate folklore, defended with total sincerity by experienced practitioners.

**The highest-leverage intervention is shortening the loop, not adding hours.** This is why immediate feedback is a defining condition of [[Deliberate Practice]] rather than an optional extra. If you can convert a slow signal into a fast proxy — predict the outcome in writing, then score yourself; get a critique before the result rather than after — you have changed the learning rate itself.

For knowledge work the cheapest fast proxy is self-testing, since it produces an immediate correctness signal that reading never does. The habits in [[Using the Testing Effect Day to Day]] are essentially latency reduction applied to studying.

There is a systems version of the same fact: a loop with a delay does not just learn slowly, it oscillates, overcorrecting because the response to the last action has not arrived — see [[Delays Cause Oscillation]].

And when a skill stalls for months, the first suspect should be a broken or absent signal rather than a ceiling, which is the diagnostic in [[Plateaus and Breakthroughs]]. The design response is to keep support in place only until the learner can generate their own signal, as in [[Scaffolding and Fading]].
