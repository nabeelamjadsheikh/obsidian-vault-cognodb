Kauffman's term, from biology: at any moment, the set of things that can come into existence next is not unlimited. It is exactly one step from what already exists. A molecule can only form from molecules present; a species can only evolve from a species alive. The frontier is real and it moves.

Ideas behave the same way, and this explains a fact that otherwise looks like coincidence: simultaneous invention. Calculus twice, natural selection twice, the telephone in the same week. The door had opened, and several people standing near it walked through. What looks like genius is very often adjacency plus attention.

Two consequences that changed how I work.

**Timing is not luck, it is position.** You cannot reach an idea four steps away from your current set no matter how clever you are. But you can move your position deliberately by acquiring the neighbouring pieces — which is what makes the input side of [[Creativity Is Combinatorial]] the controllable variable. Reading in a second field is not enrichment, it is relocation.

**Premature ideas are not wrong, they are unreachable.** An idea that requires three missing components will fail now and be obvious in a decade. The right response is to note it and keep the pieces where they can find each other later, which is precisely what [[The Idea Compost Heap]] is for.

The exploration mechanism is not deliberate search. The adjacent step usually appears when you stop steering, which is the phenomenon in [[Incubation and the Shower Effect]] — loose attention wanders over adjacent territory that focused attention drives straight past.

And it further deflates the romance of novelty: everything reachable was already almost there, which is the point of [[Originality Is Overrated]].
