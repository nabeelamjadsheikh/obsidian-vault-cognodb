Boredom is information. It reports that the current input is below your model's resolution — that whatever you are attending to contains no surprise, nothing your predictions do not already cover. That is a fact about the fit between you and the input, and it is genuinely useful.

Read correctly, it says one of three things:

1. **The input is too simple.** You have finished with this. Leave.
2. **The input is too complex to parse.** You have no foothold, so nothing registers as informative, and it presents as tedium rather than difficulty. Very common with badly taught material.
3. **You are wrongly framed.** The material is rich and you are approaching it in a way that makes it flat.

Three different diagnoses, one sensation, and the modern response is identical to all of them: eliminate the sensation immediately with a phone. Which is like taking a painkiller for every signal your body sends and calling it health.

The more expensive loss is the second-order one. Unfilled boredom is the raw material of undirected thought — the state in which the mind wanders across adjacent territory and returns with something, exactly as described in [[Incubation and the Shower Effect]]. Eliminate every empty moment and you eliminate the process, and you will experience the loss as having no ideas rather than as having no gaps.

Practically, boredom is when the half-formed things get turned over, which is why an hour of it usually ends with me rereading [[The Idea Compost Heap]] and finding two fragments that now belong together.

It is also the resource that play consumes: nothing playful happens in a fully scheduled day, which is why [[Play Is Serious Work]] requires protected emptiness rather than protected leisure.

And it is the ground state that curiosity grows from, since a question can only surface when there is silence enough to hear it, which is half of why [[Curiosity as the Engine]] is so much easier to sustain in an unfilled life.
