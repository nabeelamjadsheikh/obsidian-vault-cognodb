Six questions. Written down in advance because the questions I invent in the moment are always kinder than the ones I would have chosen beforehand, and a postmortem that flatters you is a diary entry.

## The questions

1. **What did this produce that a person could use?** Not what was learned, not what was built — what shipped. Answer in one sentence or admit the answer is nothing.
2. **When did I first know it was in trouble?** There is always a date, and it is always earlier than the date I admitted it.
3. **What did I do between those two dates?** This is the expensive question. Usually the answer is "worked harder on the wrong part".
4. **What grew without anyone deciding it should?** Scope, dependencies, the number of screens. See [[Scope Is a Stock]] for why I ask this in inflow-and-outflow terms rather than as a feeling.
5. **What would the two-week version have looked like?** Asked after the fact it is brutally clarifying, and it is the same instinct as [[Ship the Smallest Real Slice]] applied retroactively.
6. **What am I changing, exactly one thing?** Not three. Three changes is a way of changing nothing.

## Rules for running it

Written answers before any discussion, or the loudest first opinion becomes everyone's opinion. No blame section — I am usually the only participant, and self-blame is the least useful output available. Time-boxed to forty minutes.

Question six is the whole point, and its output goes somewhere with a review date rather than into a file that gets read once. [[Lantern — March Retro]] is the most recent time I ran this properly, and it produced exactly one change, which we are still doing.

The pattern the answers keep tracing is the one in [[Why Most Side Projects Die]]: nobody quits, the outflow just stops. I also run a cut-down version of question four on the vault itself during [[The Weekly Note Hygiene Review]].
