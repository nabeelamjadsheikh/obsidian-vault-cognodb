Stop arguing about whether the project is "too big" and draw it as a bathtub instead. Scope is a level. Ideas, discovered requirements and bug reports flow in. Shipped work flows out. The level rises when inflow exceeds outflow, and for no other reason.

Once you say it that way, several arguments that felt like judgement calls turn into arithmetic. The vocabulary is straight out of [[Stocks and Flows]], and the reason it transfers so cleanly is that scope behaves like every other accumulator: it has memory, it responds to rates rather than events, and it cannot be changed instantly no matter how strongly anyone feels about it.

## What the model tells you immediately

- **You cannot fix a rising stock with a one-time cut.** Descoping is draining the tub once. If the inflow rate is unchanged the level is back in six weeks, which is exactly the pattern in every project I have abandoned.
- **The level is not the problem; the rates are.** "We have too much to do" describes the stock. Only the flows can be acted on.
- **Outflow is capped by your finishing rate, not your working rate.** Work that is ninety percent done has not left the tub. This is the case for [[Ship the Smallest Real Slice]] restated in plumbing terms: a thin thing that ships is outflow, a thick thing that nearly ships is not.
- **Small persistent imbalances beat dramatic ones.** Two extra items a week and one shipped is fatal over a quarter, and invisible over a fortnight.

## The uncomfortable implication

The inflow you most need to control is your own good ideas, not other people's requests. In my own logs, requirements I discovered outnumber requirements anyone asked for by about four to one.

This is the mechanism underneath [[Why Most Side Projects Die]], and it is why question four of [[Project Postmortem Template]] asks what grew rather than how it felt.
