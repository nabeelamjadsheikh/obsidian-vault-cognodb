Everything I keep forgetting between services, written down once so I stop looking it up on forums where half the answers are people arguing about wax.

## Chain wear

Check with a proper gauge, not the ruler method — the ruler method is fine for telling you it is far too late. Numbers I go by:

- Under 0.5% — fine, keep riding.
- 0.5% to 0.75% — replace the chain now. The cassette survives.
- Over 0.75% — the chain has already worn the cassette to match it. Replace both, and expect the chainrings to be next.

Checking takes fifteen seconds and I do it on the first of the month. This is entirely a delay problem: the damage accumulates invisibly for months and the bill arrives all at once.

## Intervals that have held up

- Chain: roughly every 3,000 km in dry riding, closer to 1,800 through a wet winter.
- Cassette: every third chain, if the chains were replaced on time.
- Chainrings: when shifting under load starts skipping and a new chain does not fix it.
- Jockey wheels: annually, and they are cheap enough that there is no reason to defer.

## Degreaser

Citrus degreasers smell pleasant and do very little on a properly filthy chain. What actually works is a mineral-spirit-based degreaser in a chain bath, run twice, then a full dry before lubricating. Do not use it on sealed bearings — it will strip the grease out of the pulleys and you will hear about it within a week.

Wet lube for winter, dry lube for summer, and the important part is not which lube but wiping the excess off. Excess lube is a magnet for grinding paste.

Rewaxing: tried it for a season. Genuinely cleaner, genuinely quieter, and the setup faff means I will not keep doing it.
