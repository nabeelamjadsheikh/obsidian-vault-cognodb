For thirty days in May I removed every feed: no social apps on the phone, no news sites, no video recommendations, RSS reduced to eleven blogs I read deliberately. I kept messaging, email, and one podcast. I wrote down what happened rather than what I expected to happen, because I already knew what I expected.

## What actually changed

- **Days one to four were bad.** Genuinely bad. Reaching for a phone that had nothing on it, dozens of times a day. The reach is more automatic than I had believed.
- **Week two produced boredom**, which turned out to be the useful part — see [[Boredom Is a Signal]] for where that went.
- **Reading capacity came back.** Not immediately, and not to the level I remember from before smartphones, but I finished four books in a month having finished two in the previous four.
- **Writing improved more than reading did.** Sentences arrived whole again instead of in fragments. That is the single result that made this permanent.

## What I missed, honestly

Two things. I lost the ambient sense of what people I know are doing, and the small connective messages that came out of it did not happen. And I was genuinely out of the loop on one professional development that mattered, which cost me a week.

The second is fixable with a weekly deliberate check. The first is a real cost and I have not found a substitute for it.

The mechanism was structural rather than moral, which is the whole point of [[The Attention Economy]] — I did not become more disciplined, I made the drain physically unavailable. The permanent version is described in [[Solitude and the Input Diet]]. And the log itself, kept daily, was the thing that made this an experiment rather than an intention, which is an argument for [[Journaling as Practice, Not Record]].
