For about a year I could not tell whether I was practising [[Premeditatio Malorum]] or simply worrying with better branding. The content is identical — imagine the thing you fear, in detail, on purpose. From outside they are indistinguishable. From inside they run in opposite directions.

The difference is termination.

The Stoic rehearsal has a defined end state. You imagine the loss, you follow it through to the point where you observe that you would still be able to act well inside it, and then you stop. There is a conclusion, and the conclusion is a version of [[The Dichotomy of Control]]: this was never mine, and here is what would remain that is.

Anxiety has no end state. It runs the scenario, arrives at the dread, and restarts, because the dread is what it is optimising for even though it does not know that. You can tell which one you are in by asking a simple question: *am I closer to a conclusion than I was ten minutes ago?* If the answer is no on the third pass, this is not an exercise.

## Tests I use

- Is there a specific scenario, or a diffuse cloud? Exercises are concrete. Anxiety is vague and resists being pinned to particulars.
- Does it resolve into an action or an acceptance? If neither, abort.
- Would I schedule it? I can put visualisation in the calendar. Nobody schedules worry.

[[Voluntary Discomfort]] is a useful cross-check, because bodily exercises terminate on their own — the shower ends. That gives you a felt template for what a completed rehearsal is supposed to feel like.

[[What the Stoics Got Wrong]] belongs here too. The tradition assumes the assent is available to interrupt. Sometimes it is not, and pretending otherwise turns a technique into a reproach.
