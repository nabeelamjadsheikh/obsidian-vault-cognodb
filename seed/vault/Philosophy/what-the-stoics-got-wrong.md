I use this tradition daily and I think three of its load-bearing claims are false or unavailable.

**The physics is gone.** The whole system rests on a rational, providential cosmos in which everything that happens is part of an order that is, correctly understood, good. Strip that out and "live according to nature" loses its referent — nature is no longer prescribing anything. Modern Stoicism mostly does strip it out and then keeps the conclusions, which is helping yourself to the fruit of an argument you have discarded.

**"Externals do not matter" is easier from a palace.** Marcus was emperor. Seneca was among the richest men in Rome and made a considerable part of that money in ways he would not have wanted examined. The doctrine that health, wealth and status are indifferent is a defensible philosophical position and also an extraordinarily convenient one for those who have them. Epictetus is the exception that proves how much the biography matters.

**The account of grief is inhuman.** The claim that sorrow is a false judgement, correctable in principle, does not survive contact with actual bereavement. [[Stoicism Is Not Suppression]] is right that the target is the judgement rather than the feeling — but some judgements are simply true. The person is gone and that is bad. There is no assent to withhold.

Adjacent to that: the technique assumes the interruption point is reachable. Sometimes it is not, and the tradition has no vocabulary for that failure except insufficiency, which is where [[Negative Visualisation Versus Anxiety]] turns from a tool into a reproach.

What survives is the practical core — [[Philosophy as a Way of Life]] and the concrete duties of [[Epictetus on Roles]]. That is a smaller inheritance than the enthusiasts claim, and it is still worth having.
