Solitude is not being alone in a room. It is being free of other people's minds. You can have it on a crowded train and lose it entirely in an empty house with headphones on. The relevant variable is whether anyone else's thinking is currently arriving.

I underrated this for years because I treated input as costless. More reading is more learning; more podcasts on the walk is more value per hour. The error is that inputs do not just add material, they crowd out the process that turns material into conclusions. Nothing gets metabolised while something is arriving.

## The diet, as currently practised

- One walk a day with nothing playing. This is where roughly half the useful thoughts of the last year have come from.
- Eleven feeds, chosen once. Anything not on the list has to be sought deliberately.
- A reading-to-writing ratio I actually watch. If I have read for a week and written nothing, the ratio is wrong regardless of how good the reading was.
- No input in the first hour awake. That hour belongs to whatever the night assembled.

The thirty-day version that established all of this is in [[A Thirty-Day Input Experiment]]; this note is the steady state that survived it.

The theoretical justification is [[Attention Is a Moral Act]] — if attention builds the person, then unchosen inputs are unchosen construction. The practical justification is smaller and more convincing: I have never once had an original thought while consuming.

The hard part is that the empty stretches feel like waste while they are happening. They are not, but the feeling is real, and the correct response to it is in [[Boredom Is a Signal]] rather than in finding something to listen to.
