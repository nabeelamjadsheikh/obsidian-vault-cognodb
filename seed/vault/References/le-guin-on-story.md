A collecting note. Passages gathered over a couple of years where Le Guin makes the same argument in different registers: fiction is a thought experiment, not a prophecy, and the people who read science fiction as prediction have misunderstood the instrument.

The clearest statement is in her introduction to *The Left Hand of Darkness*, where she says outright that she is not predicting a future of ambisexual humans; she is describing, in the way novelists describe, what is already the case, by removing one variable and watching what happens to the rest. Extrapolation is the mode of the futurist. The novelist is running an experiment on the present.

> I am not predicting, or prescribing. I am describing. I am describing certain aspects of psychological reality in the novelist's way, which is by inventing elaborately circumstantial lies.

From *The Carrier Bag Theory of Fiction* comes the structural half of the argument. If the first cultural tool was a container rather than a weapon, then the oldest story shape is gathering, not conflict — which is why [[The Carrier Bag Theory of Fiction]] reads as a rebuke to every writing course that defines story as escalating conflict toward climax.

Two consequences I keep returning to. First, a novel makes its case by staging rather than asserting, which is the whole distinction in [[Narrative Versus Argument]] — she cannot be paraphrased without loss, and that is a feature. Second, the reason her essays and her fiction sound like one person is exactly the thing described in [[Voice Is What Survives Translation]]: an attention that keeps turning toward what a system costs the people inside it.
