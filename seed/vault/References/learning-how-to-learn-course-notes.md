Condensed from the four weeks of Oakley's course. I kept these rather than deleting them after the certificate because the metaphors are unusually sticky, and sticky metaphors are worth more than accurate ones you cannot recall.

**Week 1 — two modes.** The pinball table with tight and loose bumpers. Focused attention runs on grooved paths; the diffuse state wanders wide and finds new ones. You cannot occupy both at once, which is why the shower solves the problem the desk could not. Everything I later wrote in [[Diffuse and Focused Modes]] started here.

**Week 2 — memory and procrastination.** Working memory as four slots on a wobbly blackboard; long-term memory as a warehouse you must deliberately index. The forgetting demo — a page recalled at 90% after ten minutes and 40% the next day — is the version of [[The Forgetting Curve]] I still picture. The procrastination model is neat: the cue triggers genuine pain in the pain centres, and the pain stops once you start, so the intervention is the ritual of starting.

**Week 3 — chunks and competence.** Reread and it feels easy. Close the book and try to reproduce the argument and it doesn't. She uses this to sell [[Retrieval Practice Beats Review]] harder than any textbook does, because the demonstration is personal and immediate.

**Week 4 — building a schedule.** Interleave problem types. Hard tasks first thing. And [[Spaced Repetition]] not as an app but as a habit of revisiting on days two, seven and twenty-one.
