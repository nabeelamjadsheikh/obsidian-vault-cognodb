Two ways of putting things on the internet, and almost everything is the first.

**The stream** is chronological. Posts, threads, feeds, newsletters. Each item is timestamped, addressed to the moment, and structurally disposable — nobody goes back. The stream rewards frequency and reaction, and its unit is the update.

**The garden** is topological. Items sit next to related items, get revised in place, and have no publication date because they were never published in that sense. The garden rewards tending, and its unit is the idea.

The distinction is not about platform. You can garden on a blog and stream in a wiki. It is about whether the thing has a *before* and *after* or only a *when*.

## Why I want the second

Because the stream never accumulates. Ten years of posting produces ten years of posts, not a body of work — the hundredth take on a subject sits in a different location from the first ninety-nine and is not informed by them. Whereas a garden's hundredth revision *is* the previous ninety-nine, compressed.

This is the same commitment as [[Evergreen Notes]], scaled up from a single note to a whole public surface, and it is what makes [[Writing in Public]] more difficult than it looks — the audience expectations attached to publishing are all stream expectations, and satisfying them corrodes the garden.

The navigational cost is real. A stream has an obvious entry point, which is the top. A garden has none, which is why it needs hand-built paths through it; see [[Maps of Content]].

And a garden is only worth tending if it eventually talks back, which is the criterion in [[The Vault as a Thinking Partner]]. A tidy garden nobody walks in is just a stream with better filing.
