Twenty minutes, Sunday morning, coffee. Without it a vault becomes an attic: full of things that were worth keeping at the moment of keeping and have not been looked at since.

The review is a fixed checklist, deliberately short enough that I do not negotiate with it.

- [x] **Empty the inbox.** Everything in [[The Writing Inbox]] gets promoted, merged, or deleted. Nothing carries over twice; if it survives two reviews unprocessed it was never an idea.
- [x] **Orphans.** Notes with no inbound links, oldest first. Each one gets a link from somewhere or gets deleted. The heuristic for finding them is in [[Detecting Orphan Notes]], which turned out to be less trivial than it sounds because stubs and hubs both distort the count.
- [x] **Stubs older than a month.** A stub is a promise. Unkept for four weeks, the promise was not real.
- [ ] **Duplicates.** Two notes making the same claim under different titles. This is the one I skip most often and it is the one that does the most damage.
- [x] **Oversized notes.** Anything past about four hundred words gets the test in [[When to Split a Note]].

The point is not tidiness. It is that [[Evergreen Notes]] requires revisiting, and revisiting does not happen spontaneously — I have tried, for years, and it does not. The review is the only mechanism that makes the "written to be edited" claim true rather than aspirational.

What I resist is letting the review expand. Twenty minutes on maintenance is proportionate; two hours is [[Against the Perfect System]] in its most seductive form, because it feels exactly like work. The checklist itself, and the conventions it enforces, are written down in [[Vault Conventions]] so that the review does not become a weekly redesign.
