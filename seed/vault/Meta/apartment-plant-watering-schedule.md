Sunday morning, before coffee. The whole round takes about eight minutes and the only reason it works is that it is attached to something I do anyway.

## Weekly

- **Monstera** (living room, east window) — thorough soak until it runs out the bottom, then nothing until the following Sunday. Wipe the leaves once a month; it visibly cares.
- **Basil** (kitchen sill) — genuinely thirsty, sometimes needs a midweek top-up in summer. Pinch the flowers off or it goes bitter within a fortnight.
- **Calathea** (bathroom) — the humidity in there does most of the work, but it wants filtered water. Tap water browns the leaf edges within about three weeks. I tested this by accident.

## Fortnightly

- **Snake plant** (hallway, no direct light) — almost impossible to kill by neglect, very possible to kill by attention.
- **ZZ plant** (bedroom corner) — same. Both of these get skipped in winter entirely, roughly November to February.
- **Rubber plant** (living room) — top two inches of soil dry before watering, which works out fortnightly in practice.

## The one that keeps dying

- **Fiddle leaf fig.** Third one. West window, watered fortnightly, not moved, not overwatered as far as I can tell. Leaves brown from the edge inward and drop from the bottom. Current theory is a draught from the balcony door, since the damage is worst on that side. Moving it two feet in April; if this one dies the corner is getting a lamp and a bookshelf.

Rotate everything a quarter turn each week. Feed March through September only, half strength, first Sunday of the month.
