Not to remember more. I have a search bar for that, and my memory was never the bottleneck. I keep one because it produces connections I would not have made deliberately, and because those connections are the only part of thinking I have never been able to force.

The mechanism is unglamorous. Every note has to be filed next to something, and the act of choosing what it goes next to is a small forced comparison. Most comparisons yield nothing. Occasionally one yields a note about anger sitting adjacent to a note about reinforcing loops, and something happens that no amount of staring at either would have produced.

Luhmann described this as being surprised by his own system, and [[Luhmann's Second Memory]] is my attempt to work out what structural property made that possible. Short version: he let it develop rather than organising it.

## What I am not doing

- Not building a personal encyclopaedia. Completeness is not a goal and pursuing it wrecks the thing — see [[Against the Perfect System]].
- Not archiving what I read. Quotes without processing are inert.
- Not optimising retrieval. If I can only find a note by remembering it exists, the vault has failed differently.

Two constraints do most of the work. [[Atomic Notes]] keeps each note small enough to be linked into contexts I have not thought of yet. [[Links Over Folders]] stops me from committing to a single answer about where an idea belongs, which is almost always the wrong commitment made too early.

And the notes have to be the kind you return to and edit. [[Evergreen Notes]] is that discipline; without it this is just a very slow diary with an unusual filing system.
