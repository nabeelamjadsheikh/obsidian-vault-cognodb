One file. Everything unprocessed goes in it: half-sentences from the walk, a quote I want to argue with, a link, a note title with nothing under it yet. No structure, no tags, no decision about where it belongs.

The reason for the single queue is that filing is a decision and decisions are expensive at exactly the moment when capture needs to be free. If I have to choose a folder before I can write down the fragment, I will not write down the fragment. Most of what I have lost, I lost at that step.

So capture is unblocked and filing is deferred to a scheduled moment — the weekly pass in [[The Weekly Note Hygiene Review]]. Every item gets one of three outcomes:

1. **Promote.** It is a real idea and gets its own note, written properly, per [[Evergreen Notes]].
2. **Merge.** It belongs inside something that already exists, so it goes there and the fragment dies.
3. **Delete.** This is most of them, and being ruthless about it is what keeps the inbox from becoming a second vault.

The rule that makes it work is that nothing survives two reviews. An item that I could not decide about twice is not an idea, it is a mood.

The failure mode I watched myself fall into for a year: an inbox that never emptied, which is just an unsorted vault with an optimistic name. The fix was not a better inbox. It was the standing appointment.

Things that are not ideas but might become ideas do not go here — they go in [[The Idea Compost Heap]], which has no processing obligation at all and is where fragments are allowed to rot productively for months.
