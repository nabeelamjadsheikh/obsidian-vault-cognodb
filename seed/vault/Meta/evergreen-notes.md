The distinction that matters is not between good notes and bad notes. It is between notes written to be revisited and notes written to be logged.

A logged note is complete at the moment of writing. It records what happened or what a book said, and its relationship to you is finished. A vault full of these accumulates without compounding — you get more of them every month and they are not worth more than they were.

An evergreen note is written on the assumption that you will come back and change it. It states a claim in your own words, in the present tense, under a title that is itself an assertion. When you later find a counterexample, you edit the note rather than writing a second note that contradicts the first.

## Practical markers

- **The title is a claim, not a topic.** "Interleaving Beats Blocking", not "notes on interleaving".
- **It is written for a stranger**, because in eighteen months that is who you are.
- **It gets denser over time.** If a note has not changed in two years, it is either finished or dead, and those look identical from outside.

The revisiting has to be scheduled or it does not happen. That is the entire justification for [[The Weekly Note Hygiene Review]] — twenty minutes finding the notes that are drifting toward log status and either promoting them or deleting them.

Evergreen status also depends on the note being about one thing, since a note arguing three claims cannot be cleanly revised. [[Atomic Notes]] is the precondition.

The clearest failure case is source notes. It is very easy to write a summary of a book and call it permanent when it is only a record — the distinction is [[Literature Notes Versus Permanent Notes]]. And nothing arrives evergreen; it arrives as a fragment in [[The Writing Inbox]] and gets promoted on purpose.
